SIG. 04 — THE BRIEF

Let the animals do the talking.

Tbilisi Zoo has been open since 1927 — the oldest zoo in the Caucasus, and for most Georgians the first place they ever stood eye to eye with something wild. Its identity had not aged as well as its institution: a photographic logo that reproduced badly, signage accumulated one repair at a time, and merchandise that borrowed whatever style the supplier had in stock that year.

The brief was precise. Playful and family-facing, without leaning on animal photography. A mark and pattern system flexible enough to carry across bags, t-shirts, badges, tickets and signage. Georgian first — the logotype had to be drawn for the Georgian alphabet, not transliterated into it. And everything had to survive the cheapest reproduction the gift shop could buy: one colour, screen-printed on canvas.

So the strategy wrote itself as a refusal: no photographs, no cartoon mascot, no crest. The animals become paper cut-outs — flat, confident shapes a four-year-old can name at a glance — and the whole institution speaks in their silhouettes.

“No photographs, no mascots — the animals themselves, cut clean out of the paper.”

SIG. 07 — THE SYSTEM

Identity anatomy

1.1 — MARK CONSTRUCTION

Golden-section circles. Three animals share the negative space — the elephant's trunk draws the rhino's horn draws the tur's brow. Clear space = one leaf.

1.2 — TYPE SPECIMEN

A custom Georgian display face, drawn letter by letter for the logotype — all 33 letters and numerals, round and animal-fat, so the wordmark and the creatures read as one species.

LATIN SUPPORT — “TB ZOO” · SIGNAGE & TICKETS SET IN THE HOUSE GROTESQUE
